Performance
Speed: 5 mph on land, 1 mph on water
Battery Life: 24 hours
Size: 12 in by 12 in
Weight: 2-10 lbs

This profile is a very accomplishable rover upgrade from our VEX prototype. The first step would be making it more reliable and consistent at accomplishing the goals of the prototype. For example, by using larger rubber tires, the rover would not bottom out on the tiniest of hills. A lightweight profile would not require super powerful motors and make floatation easier. I noticed that our rover was very prone to getting stuck on vegetation and had almost no power.

Survivability
A wetlands rover will come across many foreign objects and environments in the wild and so it should be both dust tight and able to be submerged in water. A wetlands rover should have an IP68 ingress protection rating. The 6 indicates the object is dust tight and the 8 indicates it can handle continuous submersion in water. A 9 IP rating for water would mean it can handle high pressure jets which the rover most likely will not encounter.

Ruggedness
The rover should comply with MIL-STD-810G/H. Within this standard, there are three tests the rover should pass. First, it should pass 514.8 Vibration by testing frequencies from 4 to 2000 Hz to simulate rough terrain. The rover must endure shock impacts from drops and rough handling typical of field deployment which is defined by 516.8 Shock Testing. The rover should be able to withstand high and low temperatures as the environment can get unpredictably warm and cold. These standards are 501.7 High Temperature and 502.7 Low Temperature.

Remote Piloting Method & Features
A wetlands rover may have multiple purposes but to perform research in hard to access areas, a Direct Remote Control would perform the best. Possible features include:
2.4 GHz wireless controller with 500m range
1080p waterproof camera with live video feed to operator
Real-time telemetry (battery level, signal strength, temperature)
GPS
A 500m range could easily be upgraded depending on the task at hand. A 1080p camera enables the operator to see where they are driving the rover and real-time telemetry can allow flagging of issues before it is too late. For example, going around a certain obstacle could cause a severe drop in signal which could be detected by the telemetry and accounted for before the signal drops completely. A GPS module allows the rover to log its location for better analysis of its performance and keeping history of missions. A return-to-home feature is not necessary because the operator chooses where it goes.

Components
As VEX parts are manufactured for a controlled environment where it does not encounter any water or extreme temperatures. To replace the VEX motors, Maxon RE 25 12V brushed DC motors are a great replacement. They stall at 12W and are rated to IP67 and can be submerged for a meter of water. The Maxon brand is an industry standard and has been proven in water environments. While being 4x the cost, these provide the necessary power to propel a small rover through a complex environment. Larger rubber offroad tires instead of the plastic tracks used for our prototype would also allow the rover to function better. Another component is Spektrum DXe 2.4GHz transmitter with AR500 waterproof receiver. These components are just examples of radio components that can replace the VEX radio systems. These can be upgraded for range as needed.

Material Selection
The VEX prototype mainly used steel and pool noodles to float in water. This is a very limiting profile for a rover that needs to be maneuverable. Therefore, lightweight material such as carbon fiber or aluminium will allow the rover to float very well. An alternative to pool noodles is a ballast balloon or container. These are enclosed containers that can fill with water or stay filled with air making the rover to float. The tires can also have slight flaps that propel the rover in water. The enclosure would most likely be a box with rounded edges that sits pretty high to not get tangled in the vegetation.
